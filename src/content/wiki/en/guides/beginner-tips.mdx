## 1. Read the forest with scent tracking

Your most important early tool isn't a claw — it's your nose. **Scent tracking** lets you follow smells through the undergrowth to find prey, paths, and points of interest. Cats who track routinely finish quests faster and rarely wander into a [predator](/bosses/predators-and-battles/) blind.

## 2. Take the vertical route

**Climbing** turns the territories into three dimensions: vantage points reveal what's ahead, and high routes often skip dangerous ground. If a direct path looks rough, look up before you look around.

## 3. Hunt every excursion

Prey is everywhere, and hunting is how a young warrior trains between quests. Make a hunting lap part of your return trip to camp every time — the steady practice shows in your battles later.

![A young warrior cat stalks through ferns, tail low, eyes fixed on the trail ahead](/images/articles/wc-beginner-tips.jpg)

## 4. Learn stance-based combat before it matters

Battles are turn-based **3v3** fights built on stances, and your cat grows into new [combat moves](/items/combat-moves/) as it gains experience — 75 unique moves exist to discover. Early fights are forgiving; use them to learn how stances interact before the story stops pulling punches. The [combat system guide](/guides/combat-system/) breaks the mechanics down.

## 5. Spend materials on your den

Territory exploration yields materials, and materials make your den yours. Decorating isn't just cosmetic pride — a den you care about is one you revisit, and friends visiting asynchronously will see it too. The [crafting and den guide](/items/crafting-and-den/) explains what to gather and where to use it.

<Callout type="tip">None of these five systems costs anything but attention. Stack all five habits from your first session and rank ceremonies arrive noticeably sooner. For the bigger picture — campaign structure, modes, and Clan choice — the [beginner's guide](/guides/getting-started/) is the place to start.</Callout>
